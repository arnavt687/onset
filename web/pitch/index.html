Onset

A one-minute check-in you FaceTime, prescribed for the 90 days after a stroke or TIA. It runs the FAST checks against the patient's own baseline.

Smile · arms · speech 90-day remote monitoring Not a medical device
The weeks after hospital discharge are a high-risk window for another stroke: nearly one in four US strokes are recurrent. Onset is a FaceTime-native agent for stroke and TIA survivors in the 90 days after discharge, prescribed by the discharging care team as a daily one-minute check-in. It runs the checks of FAST, the public stroke-recognition checklist, and compares you with yourself. (Hook, 20 s. Then go straight to the phone for call 1. If the projector fails, say the hook anyway.)

After a stroke or a TIA, people go home and the checking falls to them and their family. The CDC's stroke facts page says about 185,000 US strokes a year, nearly one in four, are in people who have had a previous stroke. Our user is the survivor in those first 90 days home. The insight

Compare you with yourself

FAST checks face, arms and speech. A video call can measure all three. Compare each person with their own baseline, over FaceTime.

FAST is the public stroke-recognition checklist: ask the person to smile, raise both arms, repeat a simple phrase, and call 911 if any sign shows (CDC, Signs and Symptoms of Stroke, https://www.cdc.gov/stroke/signs-symptoms/index.html). Onset runs those checks and compares the numbers with the same person on well days, because people differ: some smiles are naturally uneven, some people speak slowly. Why now: landmark models run in real time on a laptop CPU, an agent can hold a real FaceTime line, and many older patients already know FaceTime, so there is no app to install.

    FaceTime → Chert → LiveKit → agent. MediaPipe and Scribe measure. Deterministic rules compare each metric with the baseline.

    Chert gives the agent a real Apple FaceTime line; a Mac on Chert's side bridges FaceTime into a LiveKit Cloud room. The agent is Python on livekit-agents. Smile: MediaPipe Face Landmarker, how far each mouth corner lifts, as an asymmetry index. Arms: MediaPipe Pose, wrist drift over 10 seconds measured against the shoulders, so phone wobble cancels. Speech: ElevenLabs Scribe transcribes the repeated sentence; we compute word error rate, speaking rate and the longest pause. All of this runs on CPU; face landmarks take about 9 ms a frame and pose about 16 ms on our laptop. The rules in onset/decide.py compare today with the person's baseline. The voice is ElevenLabs.

    "Face landmarks, on the laptop's CPU. It measures how far each corner of the mouth lifts." 0 means both corners lift equally, 1 means one side did not move.     Never

    It never tells anyone they're fine

    You're fine.
    No change from your usual check-in.

    The best result Onset gives, word for word: "No change from your usual check-in. If you feel unwell, call 911."

    onset/script.py · CLOSE_NO_CHANGE

    "Listen to what it says. Not 'you're fine'. No change, compared with your usual check-in." The decision module's tests fail if any of its output or string literals contains words like "fine", "normal" or "stroke". It never names a condition and never diagnoses.

    Safety by design

    Any change: caregiver joins, 911 advised

    Reposition, then retry. Face or arms out of frame: Onset says so and re-runs the step.
    Persistent failure escalates. Unclear after a retry is handled like a change.
    Sensitivity over specificity. A false positive costs a phone call.
    Longitudinal record. Every session's recordings and metrics, trended over 90 days for the care team.

    "If you feel unwell, call 911."
    "Three choices. The decision is fixed rules you can read, with no LLM in the decision path. Anything unclear counts as a change, so it leans toward false alarms. And it never tells anyone they're fine, and never diagnoses." Three outcomes: no change detected, change, unclear. Unclear is treated exactly like a change. A false alarm costs a phone call; a miss can cost far more. The caregiver text is extra and never the only safety action. Recordings and baselines stay on the machine, owner-only.

    Limits and roadmap

    What it can't do yet

    Thresholds are provisional. No accuracy numbers until a clinician-led study measures them.

    Now · research prototype
    Clinician-led validation
    Scheduled check-in calls
    Caregiver texts on Chert's Shared plan
    "The thresholds are provisional, tuned on synthetic data and one person. Our next step is a clinician-led validation study." Be direct. The thresholds were set from synthetic faces, drawn figures, generated speech and one person. We quote no accuracy numbers. Known limits: lighting and framing affect the measurements; speech recognition quietly "corrects" mildly slurred words, so word error rate alone misses mild slurring, which is why rate and pauses are also checked. Roadmap: Onset calls the person at a set time (Chert's outbound calls API), caregiver texts at any time on Chert's Shared plan, and possibly an LLM only for off-script replies, never for the decision.
